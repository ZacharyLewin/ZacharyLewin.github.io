ClimbCoach

A camera on the climbing wall that watches you climb, scores the attempt out of 100, and tells you exactly what to work on.

By Zachary D. Lewin · Montréal · Python, computer vision
“I’ve always loved climbing, a sense of freedom that is rare to find elsewhere for me. I now climb competitively in Montréal, and wanted something that allowed me to excel on my own terms…”

This is how a lot of climbers get better: watch, guess, write it down, try again. Filming yourself on a phone helps a little, but the footage tells you nothing on its own, and a good coach is expensive and rare in most gyms.

ClimbCoach is the attempt to make that page write itself. The long-term idea is a camera built into the LED training boards climbers already use. You pick a problem in the board’s app, climb it, and get back a score, a replay with your movement drawn over it, and a few specific tips, each pinned to the moment in the video it refers to.

A camera fixed to the wall has an advantage a phone never will: it knows exactly which holds are on the problem, the angle of the board and where it is looking from. Every attempt arrives already labelled with the problem, its grade and whether it was sent.

How it works

Not one black box, but a chain of small models, each of which can be checked on its own:

01

Pose

Track the climber’s joints in every frame of the video.

02

Wall

Map the camera image onto the board’s known hold layout.

03

Moves

Turn the climb into a sequence of hands and feet on specific holds.

04

Compare

Match it against good ascents of the same problem, adjusted for height and reach.

05

Score & coach

Hip path, pauses, straight arms, foot readjustments: a score out of 100, and tips.

Because the score is built from things that can be measured, every piece of advice can point to a moment in the replay. “Your hips wandered” comes with the second it happened.
